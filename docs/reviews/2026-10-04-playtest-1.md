**The arithmetic of the first turbine** (design §6, `src/game/tuning.ts`,
`catalogue.ts`, `sites.ts`):
- *Cost and loan:* a 600 kW turbine at Thy (wind ×1.3) costs €600k in 1995.
  The 80% loan of €480k at 8% over 15 years costs about €56k a year.
- *Income:* it makes about 1,570 MWh a year at the €80 tariff, so about
  €126k. O&M and land take about €17k.
- *Net:* about €53k a year. The next €120k of equity therefore takes about
  2.3 game years, roughly **5½ minutes at ×1**.
- *After the tariff:* when the tariff ends in 2005, the same turbine earns
  the placeholder hourly price. It loses money until its loan ends.

**What that shows:**
1. **Building is the only verb, and a savings timer gates it.** The player
   can only fast-forward, exactly as Lukas describes.
2. **After 2003 the cautious bot never builds again** on either seed. That
   is 22 game years, about 50 minutes at ×1, of watching cash.
3. **Only maximum leverage works, and only on some seeds.** "It is quite
   hard", and the one dynamic is income against expenses.

The placeholder 1999–2003 prices (about €16–20/MWh) make this harsher than
the real data will. The real calibration (Phase 1 part b) changes the
levels, not the structure.

## Diagnosis: five root causes

| | Root cause | Lukas's words |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Equity is the only bottleneck, and waiting is the only way through it.** One turbine's net cash buys the next one 2–3 game years later. | "you just speed up the game until you have some cash"; "very hard to afford the more expensive setups" |
| B | **There is no operating loop.** Once built, an asset has no dials: no maintenance, faults, upgrades or operating choices. The design deliberately automated operations (the Victoria 3 lesson) and overshot: nothing sits between "build" and "wait". | "nothing to do … other than waiting"; "very passive"; "options to manage the windfarms" |
| C | **The strategy is one-dimensional:** more MW is better. Apart from debt there is no risk the player chooses, so no playstyle. | "the only dynamics … expenses vs. income"; "risk loving … risk averse" |
| D | **The player cannot see what an asset really earns.** The assets table shows revenue and value, but not interest, repayments, O&M or return on equity per asset. A player who cannot see cannot learn, and choices feel arbitrary. | "more stats on how much the individual assets actually earn based on also debt interest" |
| E | **The real tools for capital are missing.** Danish wind was built with co-op shares, partners, project finance and develop-to-sell. The prototype has bank loans only. | "very hard to afford" |

Pacing amplifies A. With nothing to do, ×10 is the only sensible speed, and
at ×10 the years blur: "very quickly and very slowly at the same time".

## Precedents and real-world anchors used by the design
- **Transport Tycoon:** vehicles break down unless serviced, and servicing
  intervals are set per vehicle type. OpenTTD lets players switch
  breakdowns off ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Tycoon);
  [OpenTTD wiki](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Archive/Manual/Settings/Configuring%20patches/Vehicles)).
  Maintenance is a long-proven tycoon verb, and so is the switch to turn it
  off.
- **Condition monitoring:** the gearbox is the most failure-prone
  component. In one case study a scheduled gearbox repair took 3 weeks
  instead of 10 after a fault was caught early
  ([Acoem](https://www.acoem.com/en/case-studies/wind-case-study-bearing-defect-on-2-3mw-turbine/)).
  One study finds up to 8% of direct O&M costs saved, and up to 11% less
  lost production
  ([Strathclyde](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/77376/1/Turnbull_Carroll_Energies_2021_Cost_benefit_of_implementing_advanced_monitoring_and_predictive_maintenance.pdf)).
- **Vortex generators** (an aero retrofit): roughly 1–3% more annual energy
  ([DTU](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/increase-in-the-annual-energy-production-due-to-a-retrofit-of-vor-2/);
  [Wind Power Engineering](https://www.windpowerengineering.com/upwind-solution-case-study-impact-vortex-generators-wind-turbine-blades/)).
- **Measurement campaigns:** at least one full year of on-site measurement
  before a bankable yield estimate ([ADB](https://asiacleanenergyforum.adb.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/8_Wind-Resource-Assessment-A-Key-Step-in-Wind-Projects.pdf)).
- **Develop, build, sell:** European Energy, a Danish company, develops and
  builds projects, then sells stakes to recycle capital
  ([European Energy annual report 2016](https://europeanenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/annual-report-2016.pdf)).
- **Offshore in DK1:**
  - Horns Rev 1: 160 MW, 2002, operated from Esbjerg
    ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horns_Rev_Offshore_Wind_Farm)).
  - Anholt: 400 MW. Its tender closed on 7 April 2010 with a single bid,
    from DONG ([Danish Energy Agency](https://ens.dk/en/press/tender-anholt-offshore-wind-farm);
    [Ørsted](https://orsted.com/en/company-announcement-list/2010/04/938429)).
- **Co-op ownership:** about half of Danish wind in the 1980s and early 1990s
  (report). From 2009, at least 20% of new onshore projects had to be offered
  to locals ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/policies/17800-denmark-community-ownership-of-renewables)).
