## 1. Market and policy timeline in Europe, 1990–2026

### Takeaway
Between 1990 and 2026 Europe went from national monopolies to a coupled, liberalised and increasingly renewable market, in four steps:
- **Liberalisation pioneers:** UK privatisation (1990) and the Nordic exchange (1993/1996).
- **EU unbundling directives:** 1996, 2003 and 2009.
- **Feed-in-tariff booms and their caps or retroactive cuts:** Germany from 1991/2000; Italy 2005–2013; Spain 2007–2014.
- **Market coupling:** 2006 → 2010 → 2014 → 15-minute day-ahead products in October 2025.

The 2021–22 gas crisis brought emergency revenue caps and the Iberian gas cap, then the 2024 reform that makes two-sided CfDs the default support model from about 2027. In 2025–26 the main stresses are on system stability (the Iberian blackout), a rapid rise in negative-price hours, Baltic grids leaving the Russian system, and cross-border energy-island deals.

### Cited Findings

#### 1990–1999: liberalisation and the first feed-in law
- **31 Mar 1990, England & Wales.** A new structure under the Electricity Act 1989 introduced the Electricity Pool as the wholesale market. Generation was separated from transmission, allowing competition in both retail and wholesale — [EPRG Working Paper 26, Univ. of Cambridge](https://jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/eprg-wp26.pdf).
- **1991, UK.** National Power and PowerGen, a price-setting duopoly, were privatised — [EPRG WP26](https://jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/eprg-wp26.pdf). (The capacity figures quoted for the two firms vary between sources, so they are omitted here.)
- **1995, UK.** The regulator forced a first 6 GW divestiture plus a two-year price control and encouraged new independent power projects. A second divestment round followed — [ESD News, "The rise and demise of electricity competition"](https://esdnews.com.au/the-rise-and-demise-of-electricity-competition/).
- **Pool problems cited in reviews:** wholesale prices did not fall in line with generators' input costs; there was little supply-side pressure or demand-side participation; governance was inflexible — [Ofgem NETA first-year review](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/ofgem-publications/78886/review-first-year-neta-review-document-vol-1.pdf); [NAO, The New Electricity Trading Arrangements (2003)](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2003/05/0203624es.pdf).
- **1 Jan 1991, Germany.** The Stromeinspeisungsgesetz (StrEG) entered into force, described as "the first green electricity feed-in tariff scheme in the world":
  - Grid operators were obliged to buy renewable power.
  - Wind and solar were paid **90%** of the previous year's mean revenue per kWh from electricity sold through the public grid.
  - Small hydro, biomass and biogas plants under 500 kW got **75%**; plants of 500 kW–5 MW got **65%**.
  - Sources: [IEA Policies database](https://www.iea.org/policies/3477-electricity-feed-in-law-of-1991-stromeinspeisungsgesetz); [wind-works.org, Evolution of Feed-in Tariffs](https://wind-works.org/evolution-of-feed-in-tariffs/).
- **Nordic market:**
  - 1991: the Norwegian parliament decided to deregulate power trading.
  - 1993: Statnett Marked AS was set up as an independent company.
  - 1996: it became **Nord Pool** when Sweden joined (owned 50/50 by Statnett and Svenska Kraftnät), the world's first international power exchange.
  - Finland joined in 1998, West Denmark in 1999 and East Denmark in 2000.
  - Sources: [Nord Pool (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord_Pool); [Nord Pool, "Power to the people – the Nordic power market 20 years"](https://www.nordpoolgroup.com/globalassets/power-to-the-people---the-nordic-power-market-20-yrs.pdf).
- **EU directives:**
  - 96/92/EC was the first electricity directive.
  - 2003/54/EC (the second) imposed *legal* unbundling of network operators.
  - 2009/72/EC (the third, adopted 13 Jul 2009, part of the Third Energy Package) offered TSOs a choice of ownership unbundling, an independent system operator, or an independent transmission operator. DSOs still only needed legal unbundling.
  - Sources: [IAEE proceedings](https://iaee.org/en/publications/proceedingsabstractdoc.aspx?id=5078); [Directive 2009/72/EC text (legislation.gov.uk)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2009/72).
- **Danish wind cooperatives:**
  - The Danish wind-turbine owners' association was founded in 1978 (Danske Vindkraftværker, later Danmarks Vindmølleforening) and lobbied for favourable conditions.
  - Cooperatives owned about **50%** of Danish installed wind capacity from the 1980s to the early 1990s.
  - Communities had been financing village or farm turbines since the 1970s.
  - Sources: [Green Economy Coalition](https://www.greeneconomycoalition.org/news-and-resources/people-power-denmarks-energy-cooperatives); [P2P Foundation, Wind Energy Cooperatives](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Wind_Energy_Cooperatives).
- **1996, Copenhagen.** The Copenhagen Environment and Energy Office started the Middelgrunden offshore project with a local cooperative and Copenhagen Energy. The farm ended up 50% owned by about **10,000** cooperative investors (one member, one vote, regardless of shares held) and 50% by the municipal utility — [Middelgrunden wind farm (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelgrunden_wind_farm); [P2P Foundation](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Middelgrunden_Wind_Turbine_Cooperative).

#### 2000–2009: feed-in gold rush, carbon market, coupling, first negative prices
- **2000, Germany.** The EEG replaced the StrEG. Renewables got priority grid access and the payment rate was fixed for **20 years** — [wind-works.org](https://wind-works.org/evolution-of-feed-in-tariffs/); [EEG (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Renewable_Energy_Sources_Act).
- **27 Mar 2001, UK.** NETA replaced the Pool. Wholesale prices were about 40% lower than when the regulator first proposed reforming the trading system — [Project Finance Law, "Lessons from Year One" (2002)](https://www.projectfinance.law/publications/2002/april/new-uk-electricity-regulatory-regime-lessons-from-year-one); [Ofgem, NETA first three months](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2001/08/a-review-of-the-first-three-months-31-08_0.pdf).
- **2005–2007, EU ETS Phase 1.** Too many allowances were handed out, and they could not be banked into Phase 2. The price fell to about €0–1/t in 2007 — [CER (2009)](https://www.cer.eu/publications/archive/bulletin-article/2009/carbon-price-collapse-threatens-eus-climate-agenda); [arXiv 2511.04988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04988); [European Commission, Development of EU ETS 2005–2020](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/carbon-markets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets/development-eu-ets-2005-2020_en).
- **Jan 2008.** ETS Phase 2 began. With tighter allocation and partial acceptance of CDM credits, the price rebounded to about €20/t — [arXiv 2511.04988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04988).
- **Jul 2005 / Feb 2006, Italy.** The first Conto Energia decrees introduced a PV feed-in *premium* of **€0.445–0.490/kWh** — [IEA Policies database](https://prod.iea.org/policies/4902-old-feed-in-premium-for-photovoltaic-systems-i-ii-iii-iv-conto-energia).
- **Nov 2006.** Trilateral Market Coupling (TLC) linked France, Belgium and the Netherlands. The idea dated from 2003 — [EPEX SPOT, "10 years since the first market coupling initiative TLC"](https://epexspot.com/en/news/10-years-first-market-coupling-initiative-tlc).
- **2007, Spain.** Royal Decree 661/2007 set the incentives that triggered a rush of installations. €935m was committed to major plants in 2007 alone — [Renewable Energy Magazine (2017)](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/espana-pierde-su-primer-arbitraje-internacional-por-20170505).
- **1 Sep 2008.** Negative bids were first allowed on the EEX/EPEX day-ahead market. **Early October 2008** (5 Oct per the MPRA paper) brought the first negative day-ahead price in the German market area. The cause was high wind during low demand, with baseload plants bidding below cost to avoid ramping down — [MPRA paper 31834](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/31834/1/MPRA_paper_31834.pdf); [Journal of Energy Markets (2011)](https://www.doi.org/10.21314/JEM.2011.079).

#### 2010–2019: market integration, subsidy hangover, retroactivity
- **Nov 2010.** Central-West Europe (CWE) market coupling went live, adding Germany/Austria and Luxembourg, together with Interim Tight Volume Coupling to the Nordics — [TenneT, market coupling](https://www.tennet.eu/nl-en/markets/dutch-market/market-coupling); [CRE position paper on flow-based (2015)](https://www.cre.fr/fileadmin/Documents/Deliberations/import/150326_position_paper_flow_based.pdf).
- **Late 2010 → 2013, Spain.** A series of cuts to renewable remuneration: the first in late 2010 under the PSOE government, the last in the 2013 electricity-sector reform under the PP government. Order IET/221/2013 (state gazette, 16 Feb 2013) applied retroactive cuts to PV installed 2009–2011 — [Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/espana-pierde-su-primer-arbitraje-internacional-por-20170505); [pv magazine (2013)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2013/02/21/spain-publishes-retroactive-pv-fit-cuts_100010298/).
- **Italy, later Conto Energia rounds:** CE III (2010–11) paid €0.22–0.34/kWh; CE IV (2011–12) €0.17–0.27/kWh; CE V (2012–13) €0.10–0.22/kWh — [pv magazine (2012), "Is Italy's Conto Energia V pointless?"](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2012/07/17/is-italys-conto-energia-v-pointless_10007760/); [SurgePV (secondary source)](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/italy-feed-in-tariffs-rooftop-solar).
- **Apr 2013, Italy.** 16.7 GW of PV installed — [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2013/04/19/italy-now-has-16-7-gw-of-installed-solar-pv-capacity/).
- **28 May 2013, Italy.** €6.697bn of the roughly **€6.7bn per year** subsidy cap had been allocated, so Conto Energia closed in 2013 — [Solarserver (2013)](https://www.solarserver.de/2013/06/04/italiens-photovoltaik-einspeiseverguetung-wird-auslaufen-obergrenze-des-foerderprogramms-conto-energia-fast-erreicht/).
  - More than 90% of Italian PV built 2006–2016 was supported by Conto Energia.
  - The estimated lifetime cost (2006–2038) is about **€130bn** — [Univ. of Delaware study](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/8a6bec60-b379-4a38-ba91-66629e7b65e6).
- **Late 2013 onwards, Spain.** "More than four dozen" international arbitrations were filed against Spain — [Kluwer Arbitration Blog (2019)](https://arbitrationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/2019/12/30/royal-decree-law-17-2019-an-opportunity-for-spain-to-leave-behind-the-renewable-energy-arbitrations/?output=pdf).
  - **May 2017:** Spain lost the first case, Eiser Infrastructure at ICSID (filed 2013). Eiser had invested in 2007 in two 50 MW solar-thermal plants in Extremadura and Castilla-La Mancha — [Energías Renovables](https://www.energias-renovables.com/panorama/espana-pierde-su-primer-arbitraje-internacional-por-20170505); [El Independiente](https://www.elindependiente.com/economia/2017/05/05/espana-pierde-su-primer-arbitraje-ante-el-ciadi-por-los-recortes-a-las-renovables/amp/).
- **Feb 2014.** North-Western Europe price coupling (CWE, GB, Nordics, Baltics). South-Western Europe (Spain, Portugal) was coupled later in 2014 — [EPEX SPOT](https://epexspot.com/en/news/10-years-first-market-coupling-initiative-tlc); [Next Kraftwerke, market coupling](https://www.next-kraftwerke.com/knowledge/market-coupling).
- **2011, Fukushima.** Germany's nuclear phase-out, begun "more than 20 years" before 2023, was accelerated after the Fukushima disaster — [Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/germany-shuts-down-its-last-remaining-nuclear-reactors-4-gw.html); [The Energy Year](https://theenergyyear.com/news/germany-shuts-last-remaining-nuclear-plants/).

#### 2020–2026: crisis, caps, reform, stability stress
- **Aug 2021 to Dec 2021.**
  - In August 2021, German gas-fired generation fell 35% and coal rebounded as margins diverged — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/080521-german-gas-generation-falls-35-coal-rebounds-as-margins-diverge).
  - In December 2021, a gas rally pushed power prices to "unprecedented highs" — [S&P Global Platts](https://www.spglobal.com/platts/en/market-insights/latest-news/electric-power/122121-europes-energy-crisis-deepens-as-gas-rally-pushes-power-to-unprecedented-highs).
- **Apr 2022.** German power climbed above €200/MWh on gas costs — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-04-13/german-power-climbs-above-200-euros-on-higher-gas-costs).
- **Mid-June 2022, the "Iberian exception".** Spain and Portugal capped the gas price used for power generation:
  - **€40/MWh** until December 2022, then rising €5/MWh per month to May 2023.
  - Extended to **31 Dec 2023**, on a new path from €55 (March 2023) to €65 (December 2023), rising €1.10/MWh per month.
  - Power prices fell 22% on the first day.
  - Sources: [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=32916); [Columbia CGEP commentary](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Iberian-Exception_Commentary_CGEP_051723-2.pdf); [ECO News](https://econews.pt/?p=38731); [Catalan News](https://www.catalannews.com/society-science/item/cost-of-electricity-to-decrease-22-on-first-day-of-gas-price-cap); [The Olive Press (extension)](https://theolivepress.es/spain-news/2023/03/28/eu-agrees-to-extension-of-energy-price-cap-for-spain-and-portugal).
- **17 Aug 2022.** German day-ahead daily price of **€552/MWh**, a 2022 record at the time.
- **26 Aug 2022.** TTF gas record above **€300/MWh**. French next-quarter and next-year power futures exceeded **€1,100/MWh**.
  - Sources for both dates: [AleaSoft](https://aleasoft.com/price-records-european-electricity-markets-after-highs-gas/); [IEA chart, TTF Aug–Oct 2022](https://origin.iea.org/data-and-statistics/charts/day-ahead-and-month-ahead-ttf-prices-in-european-union-august-october-2022).
- **6 Oct 2022.** Council Regulation (EU) 2022/1854:
  - Capped the market revenues of "inframarginal" generators at **€180/MWh**, at least from 1 Dec 2022 to 30 Jun 2023.
  - Covered wind, solar, geothermal, hydro without reservoir, biomass (except biomethane), waste, nuclear, lignite, crude oil products and peat.
  - Surplus revenue was to go to consumers.
  - Exempt: demonstration projects and plants whose revenues were already capped (feed-in tariffs, two-way CfDs).
  - Sources: [Ashurst](https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/emergency-legal-measure-in-the-energy-sector/); [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102hzy3/the-eus-blueprint-for-a-revenue-cap-on-renewable-electricity); [EY legal update](https://ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/es-es/technical/alertas-fiscal-legal/documents/ey-22-10-17-legal-update-rule-ue-2022-1854.pdf).
- **Early 2023.** The EU carbon price topped **€100/t** — [Eka weekly roundup, 3 Mar 2023](https://ekavc.substack.com/p/ekas-weekly-roundup-3-march-2023).
- **15 Apr 2023, Germany.** The last three reactors shut down: Emsland (1.3 GW), Isar 2 (1.4 GW) and Neckarwestheim 2 (1.3 GW). They had been due to close at the end of 2022 but were kept running because of the Ukraine-war energy crisis. Germany targets 80% renewable electricity by 2030 — [Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/germany-shuts-down-its-last-remaining-nuclear-reactors-4-gw.html); [The Energy Year](https://theenergyyear.com/news/germany-shuts-last-remaining-nuclear-plants/); [Times of Israel/AP](https://www.timesofisrael.com/end-of-an-era-germany-shuts-down-its-last-3-nuclear-reactors/).
- **13 Jun 2024 (adopted) / 16 Jul 2024 (in force).** Regulation (EU) 2024/1747 reformed EU electricity market design (amending Regulations 2019/942 and 2019/943):
  - Direct price support for new low-carbon generation must use **two-way CfDs** or equivalent schemes, giving a minimum remuneration *and* a cap on excess revenue.
  - These rules apply after a **three-year transition, i.e. from about July 2027**.
  - Sources: [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/energy-infrastructure/art/reform-european-electricity-market-design); [Stiftung Umweltenergierecht (Sept 2024)](https://stiftung-umweltenergierecht.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Stiftung_Umweltenergierecht_Kamm_CfDs_New-rules-for-the-promotion-of-RES_2024-09-26.pdf); [European Parliament OEIL](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1785837).
- **5–7 Nov and 11–12 Dec 2024, German Dunkelflaute.** Day-ahead prices rose above €300/MWh at times and peaked at **€936/MWh** for 17:00–18:00 on Thursday 12 Dec 2024. A joint Bundesnetzagentur/Bundeskartellamt study (21 Oct 2025) found no abusive practices — [Bundesnetzagentur press release](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2025/20251021_Preisspitzen.html); [SMARD](https://www.smard.de/en/study-of-electricity-price-peaks-completed-218346); [Clean Energy Wire](https://www.cleanenergywire.org/news/major-utilities-cleared-price-manipulation-during-2024-dunkelflaute-periods-report).
- **8–9 Feb 2025, Baltic states.**
  - On 8 Feb, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania disconnected from the Russian-led BRELL system and ran in isolation for more than a day.
  - On 9 Feb at 14:05 EET they synchronised with Continental Europe via the LitPol Link.
  - €1.6bn was invested (75% EU co-financed): 1,400 km of high-voltage lines, 7 new substations and more than 40 refurbished.
  - Sources: [Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/109685/); [PISM](https://pism.pl/publications/baltic-states-synchronise-electricity-grids-with-the-eu); [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/distributed-energy-resources/news/55241202/the-baltic-states-to-connect-to-european-electricity-grid).
- **28 Apr 2025.** Iberian blackout — details under §3.
- **June 2025.** EU solar reached 45.4 TWh, 22.1% of net generation, up 22% on June 2024. *Caveat: the search summary did not clearly state the geography, though the figures match EU-level reporting* — [energiezukunft](https://www.energiezukunft.eu/erneuerbare-energien/solarenergie/strommarkt/positive-rekorde-fuehren-zu-negativen-preisen); [IENE Comment No. 66](https://www.iene.eu/media/IENE_Comment__No_66.pdf).
- **30 Sep 2025 trading / 1 Oct 2025 delivery.** The 15-minute market time unit went live in Single Day-Ahead Coupling across all European bidding zones. It had originally been planned for 11 Jun 2025 — [OTE](https://www.ote-cr.cz/en/about-ote/ote-news/market-coupling-steering-committee-confirms-go-live-of-15-minute-mtu-in-sdac-on-trading-day-30-september-2025-for-delivery-day-1-october-2025); [APG](https://markt.apg.at/en/news-press/sdac-go-live-of-15-minute-market-time-unit-in-day-ahead-market-coupling-successful-as-of-september-30-2025/); [NEMO Committee](https://nemo-committee.eu/assets/files/15-minute-mtu-in-sdac-was-implemented.pdf); [OKTE (rescheduling)](https://okte.sk/en/information/news/2025-05-14-press-release-announce-of-rescheduling-go-live-date-for-15-minute-mtu-in-sdac).
- **2025.** The EU installed **27.1 GWh** of new battery storage. EU battery capacity is now above **77 GWh**, ten times the 2021 level. *(Attribution best-effort.)* — [pv magazine (May 2026)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/08/europes-negative-electricity-price-hours-double-in-q1-amid-renewables-surpluses-market-imbalances/); [energiezukunft](https://www.energiezukunft.eu/erneuerbare-energien/solarenergie/strommarkt/positive-rekorde-fuehren-zu-negativen-preisen).
- **Jan 2026.** Denmark and Germany signed a bilateral agreement on the Bornholm Energy Island — details under §2.
- **20 Mar 2026.** ENTSO-E published its final report on the Iberian blackout — details under §3.
- **Q1 2026: negative prices.**
  - EU-27 day-ahead markets recorded **1,223 negative-price hours**, against 593 in Q1 2025 and 119 in Q1 2022.
  - Spain had **347** negative hours in Q1 2026, 16% of all hours, against 73 in the 2025 comparison period. Spain alone had **about 200** negative hours in April 2026.
  - Spain, Portugal and Greece drove the increase.
  - Sources: [pv magazine (8 May 2026)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/08/europes-negative-electricity-price-hours-double-in-q1-amid-renewables-surpluses-market-imbalances/); [euenergyprices.eu](https://euenergyprices.eu/en/blog/negative-electricity-prices-april-2026-record); [Euronews (5 May 2026)](https://de.euronews.com/2026/05/05/strompreise-fallen-in-europa-unter-null-warum-das-kein-gutes-zeichen-ist).

### Inferences
- **Suggested policy eras for the game** (based on the dates above):
  1. **1990–1999, "Monopolies crack."** Small producers sell under guaranteed-purchase or feed-in rules (Germany pays 90% of the retail-derived rate). Wholesale pools and exchanges exist (GB Pool, Nord Pool) but are dominated by incumbents.
  2. **2000–2009, "Feed-in gold rush."** 20-year fixed tariffs (EEG), very rich PV premiums (Italy's €0.445–0.49/kWh; Spain's RD 661), a carbon market that crashes in 2007, market coupling starting in 2006, and the first negative prices in 2008.
  3. **2010–2019, "The hangover."** Subsidy budgets hit their caps (Italy 2013) and retroactive cuts arrive (Spain 2010–2013). Investors sue and win (Eiser, 2017). Prices integrate across borders (CWE 2010, NWE/SWE 2014).
  4. **2020–2026, "Volatility."** Gas shock, windfall caps (€180/MWh), an Iberian price cap, the German nuclear exit, Dunkelflaute spikes (€936/MWh), a sharp rise in negative prices, the first system blackout of its kind (2025), 15-minute markets, and two-sided CfDs on the way (about 2027).
- **Mechanics these facts support:**
  - A per-country **"policy risk" meter** that rises with cumulative subsidy cost. Italy hit a €6.7bn/yr cap; Spain cut retroactively after its 2007–08 boom.
  - A **windfall clawback** above €180/MWh, only during Dec 2022–Jun 2023 in the history mode.
  - A **two-sided CfD** contract type, mandatory for newly subsidised builds from mid-2027.
- **Price-shape calibration:**
  - Negative prices should be impossible before late 2008 (negative bids were not allowed).
  - They should then be rare and wind-driven in Germany.
  - By the mid-2020s they should be frequent in solar-heavy markets. Spain's 16% of Q1 2026 hours is a usable high-end benchmark for a sunny southern market in spring.
- **Trading-granularity unlock:** hourly products until September 2025, then **15-minute** day-ahead products from 1 Oct 2025, which boosts the value of fast storage and flexible assets.
- **Grid-integration unlocks:** "Nordic exchange access" from 1996; "cross-border coupling" from 2006, 2010 and 2014; a "Baltic resync" scenario in 2025.

    - The EEG surcharge peaked at about 6.88 ct/kWh in 2017 and fell to zero on 1 Jul 2022.
    - The 2023 targets are 80% renewables by 2030, 215 GW of PV and 115 GW of onshore wind.
    - The coal exit is set for 2038 at the latest (2020 law).
    - Nuclear: Atomkonsens in June 2000, written into law in 2002; lifetimes extended in autumn 2010; a moratorium in March 2011 shut 8 reactors; the Bundestag voted on 30 Jun 2011 to end nuclear power by 2022.
    - The German offshore auction of August 2025 reportedly received **no bids**.
  - **Denmark:**
    - From 1979 a roughly 30% investment subsidy, phased out by 1989.
    - A utility obligation to buy wind power at about 85% of the consumer price.
    - Residency-based ownership rules.
    - The 2008 Promotion of Renewable Energy Act's offer of 20% local ownership.
    - The December 2024 3 GW offshore tender received zero bids and was relaunched in 2025 with state-backed CfDs.
  - **Spain:**
    - RD 661/2007 paid small PV about 44 c€/kWh for 25 years. About 2.6–2.7 GW of PV was installed in 2008.
    - RD 1578/2008 capped new PV at about 500 MW a year. RDL 1/2012 imposed a moratorium.
    - RDL 9/2013, Law 24/2013 and Order IET/1045/2014 replaced tariffs with a regulated "reasonable return" of about 7.398% pre-tax.
    - The "sun tax" on self-consumption (RD 900/2015) was repealed by RDL 15/2018.
    - The Eiser award was about €128m.
  - **Elsewhere:**
    - Italy held referendums against nuclear in 1987 and on 12–13 Jun 2011.
    - Switzerland: no-replacement decision in May 2011; Energy Strategy 2050 approved by referendum on 21 May 2017.
    - Belgium's parliament repealed the 2003 phase-out law in May 2025.
    - France: the ARENH mechanism (from 1 Jul 2011, €42/MWh from 2012, 100 TWh) expired on 31 Dec 2025.
    - Gas crisis: Nord Stream 1 flows halted from end-August 2022; Nord Stream sabotage on 26 Sep 2022; an EU gas price-cap mechanism (€180/MWh) was agreed in December 2022.
    - Russian gas transit through Ukraine ended on 1 Jan 2025.
- **Conflicts and uncertainty:**
  - The ETS reportedly first topped €100/t in late February or early March 2023; the exact day was not retrieved.
  - The first negative German price is variously dated 4 or 5 Oct 2008.
  - Middelgrunden's cooperative membership is given as about 10,000 by the retrieved source; other counts circulate.

## 2. Technology timeline and catalogue (first commercial year, unit size, cost trajectory; future tech with maturity)

### Takeaway
- **Unit sizes grew enormously.** Offshore turbines went from 450 kW (Vindeby, 1991) to 15 MW (He Dreiht, 2025), about 33 times larger.
- **Costs collapsed.** PV modules went from $106/W (1976) to about $0.10/W (2023). Utility PV's levelised cost fell 90% from 2010 to 2024. Battery storage system costs fell 93% (from $2,571 to $192/kWh), and stationary battery packs reached **$70/kWh in 2025**.
- **Large nuclear was the opposite story:** first-of-a-kind EPRs ran over budget by multiples. Olkiluoto 3 took 2005–2023 and about €11bn; Flamanville 3 cost €23.7bn against €3.3bn planned.
- **"Experimental" tech has a mixed record.** Successes: tidal (MeyGen), floating wind (Hywind), gravity storage (Rudong), e-methanol (Kassø). Failures: wave (Pelamis), airborne wind (Makani, Ampyx), FlagshipONE. In 2026 liquid-air storage and SMRs are at first-of-a-kind construction.

### Cited Findings

#### Onshore wind and OEM lineage
- **1979.** Vestas erected its first prototype: a V10 of 33 kW with a 10 m rotor. From 1989 it made wind turbines only — [Vestas (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestas); [lex.dk](https://lex.dk/Vestas).
- **Manufacturer lineage:**
  - Micon and NEG merged in 1997 to form NEG Micon. NEG Micon merged with Vestas in 2004 (announced 2003), creating the world's largest turbine manufacturer.
  - Bonus Energy (formerly Danregn Vindkraft, 1980–2004) was taken over by Siemens in 2004 and became Siemens Wind Power.
  - Vestas withdrew from offshore wind "for a period" in favour of Bonus/Siemens.
  - Sources: [List of Danish wind turbine manufacturers (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Danish_wind_turbine_manufacturers); [Vestas history 1999–2004](https://vestas.com/en/about/this-is-vestas/history/from-1999-2004).
- **2024 costs (IRENA).** Onshore wind was the cheapest new renewable: global weighted-average levelised cost **$0.034/kWh**, total installed cost **$1,041/kW**. The five-year outlook is about **$861/kW** — [IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024); [IRENA summary PDF](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf); [engineers.scot](https://engineers.scot/news/2025-08-18-onshore-wind-beats-solar-pv-as-the-lowest-cost-renewable-energy-source-in-2024).

#### Offshore wind (fixed-bottom)
- **1991, Vindeby (Lolland, Denmark).** The world's first offshore wind farm:
  - 11 Bonus 450 kW turbines in shallow water, **4.95 MW** in total.
  - Supplied the equivalent of 2,000–3,000 Danish households.
  - Produced **243 GWh in 25 years**. Decommissioned for cost reasons in 2017 (finished September 2017), the first wind farm DONG decommissioned.
  - Sources: [Vindeby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindeby_Offshore_Wind_Farm); [Ørsted (2017)](https://orsted.com/en/media/newsroom/news/2017/03/the-worlds-first-offshore-wind-farm-is-retiring); [CleanTechnica (2017)](https://cleantechnica.com/2017/09/11/worlds-first-ever-offshore-wind-farm-finally-dismantled).
- **2025, He Dreiht (German North Sea, EnBW).** The first **Vestas V236-15.0 MW** was commissioned here:
  - 960 MW farm of 64 turbines, about 85 km from Borkum.
  - 115.5 m blades, 43,742 m² swept area, a claimed capacity factor above 60%, about 80 GWh a year per turbine (≈20,000 households).
  - Installation began in Q2 2025, with full commissioning planned for Q4 2025. Vestas was pre-selected in July 2021.
  - Sources: [Recharge](https://www.rechargenews.com/suppliers/vestas-powers-up-first-of-its-huge-flagship-offshore-wind-turbines/2-1-1906824); [Windtech International](https://www.windtech-international.com/product-news/first-v236-15-0-mw-turbine-commissioned-offshore-at-he-dreiht-offshore-wind-farm); [CompositesWorld](https://compositesworld.com/news/vestas-to-supply-64-wind-turbines-to-he-dreiht-offshore-project-in-germany); [Windinsider (2021)](https://windinsider.com/2021/07/20/vestas-was-pre-selected-through-an-eu-tender-to-provide-v236-15-0-mw-offshore-turbines/).
- **2024 costs (IRENA).** Offshore total installed cost **$2,852/kW**; levelised cost up 4% on 2023; five-year outlook about **$2,316/kW** — [IRENA](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024).

#### Solar PV and solar thermal (CSP)
- **Module prices:** **$106/W in 1976** to about **$0.10/W**, a 99.9% fall. Prices dropped about 50% in 2023 alone. - **Utility-scale PV (IRENA):** levelised cost fell **90% between 2010 and 2024** to **$0.043/kWh**, 41% below fossil alternatives. 2024 total installed cost **$691/kW**; five-year outlook about **$388/kW** — [IRENA](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024); [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/business/irena-renewable-power-generation-costs-in-2024-report).
- **Competitiveness:** 91% of new renewable capacity added in 2024 was cheaper than fossil alternatives — [Balkan Green Energy News, IRENA](https://balkangreenenergynews.com/irena-91-of-new-renewables-units-are-more-cost-effective-than-fossil-fuel-alternatives/).
- **CSP:** levelised cost fell 46% from 2023 to 2024 (IRENA). Spanish CSP in the 2007 boom included Eiser's two 50 MW plants — [IRENA](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024); [Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/espana-pierde-su-primer-arbitraje-internacional-por-20170505).
- **Kassø Solar Park (Denmark):** 304 MW, described as Northern Europe's largest — [LEGO Group (2025)](https://www.lego.com/aboutus/news/2025/may/kass-e-methanol-facility-officially-inaugurated).

#### Batteries (electrochemical storage)
- **System costs (IRENA):** battery storage fell **93% from 2010 to 2024**, from **$2,571/kWh to $192/kWh** — [IRENA summary](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf).
- **Pack prices (BNEF survey, Dec 2025):**
  - Average lithium-ion pack **$108/kWh**, down 8% despite higher metal prices.

  - Sources: [BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/); [Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/?p=91801); [pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2025/12/10/global-lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-kwh-says-bnef).

### Inferences
- **Derived performance numbers, for balancing:**
  - Vindeby's 243 GWh over 25 years from 4.95 MW gives about 9.7 GWh/yr, a **capacity factor of about 22%** for 1991-era offshore.
  - The V236's 80 GWh/yr from 15 MW gives **about 61%**, consistent with the ">60%" claim.
  - So turbine output per unit grew roughly 180-fold (≈2.0 GWh/yr per 450 kW turbine → 80 GWh/yr), not just 33-fold in nameplate. That is a good argument for "turbine generation" tech tiers.
- **Cost per GW for large nuclear** (computed from the cited figures):

## 3. Notable real events usable as in-game events and scenarios

### Takeaway
The verified event deck covers:
- **Storms:** Lothar, 1999.
- **Cascading blackouts:** Italy 2003; the UCTE split of 2006; Iberia 2025, the first overvoltage-driven system blackout.
- **Price shocks:** the 2022 gas crisis; the Dunkelflaute of 2024.
- **Supply-side failures:** French nuclear corrosion in 2022.
- **Policy and market rule changes:** the ETS crash of 2007, Spanish retroactive cuts, the Italian subsidy cap, the 2022 revenue cap and Iberian gas cap, 2025 grid resynchronisation.
- **Industry crises:** Siemens Gamesa's quality defects, Ørsted's write-downs, stop-work order and rescue rights issue, and the failures of FlagshipONE, Pelamis, Makani and Ampyx.

Each comes with dates and magnitudes that map directly onto game effects.

### Cited Findings

#### Weather, climate and physical-system events
- **26 Dec 1999, storm Lothar** (with Martin): crossed France, Switzerland and south-west Germany at more than 120 mph.
  - More than 200 pylons toppled; more than 3 million households lost power for days.
  - Economic losses above €15bn, of which €8.6bn insured.
  - 140 deaths across Europe, 88 in France. About 200 million m³ of forest felled.
  - Sources: [Swiss Re](https://www.swissre.com/risk-knowledge/mitigating-climate-risk/winter-storms-in-europe/storms-lothar-martin-wreak-havoc-across-europe.html); [PLANAT](https://www.planat.ch/en/knowledge-base/storm/translate-to-englisch-lothar).
- **28 Sep 2003, Italy blackout:**
  - At about 03:01 the 380/400 kV Mettlen–Lavorgo (Lukmanier) line in Switzerland arced to a tree. Its flow shifted onto parallel lines through France, which overloaded and sagged into trees.
  - All of peninsular Italy lost power for about 12 hours, and part of Switzerland near Geneva for 3 hours. About 56 million people were affected; 30,000 were trapped on trains.
  - Sources: [2003 Italy blackout (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Italy_blackout); [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/environmental-emissions/swiss-point-the-finger-for-italian-blackout/); [C. Johnson, Univ. of Glasgow](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~johnson/papers/Chris_Johnson_Italian_Blackout.pdf).
- **4 Nov 2006, European system split:**
  - A planned disconnection of the Ems river line crossing in north-west Germany, to let a cruise ship pass, triggered cascading trips.
  - The UCTE grid split into three islands (West, North-East, South-East). More than 15 million customers lost power for about 2 hours.
  - It was the most severe disturbance in UCTE history; operators' fast action prevented a Europe-wide blackout.
  - Sources: [2006 European blackout (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_European_blackout); [UCTE/ENTSO-E interim report](https://eepublicdownloads.entsoe.eu/clean-documents/pre2015/publications/ce/otherreports/IC-Interim-Report-20061130.pdf); [CEER/ERGEG interim report](https://ceer.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/E06-BAG-01-05_InterimReport.pdf).
- **2022, French nuclear slump:**
  - Output fell 23% to **279 TWh**, the lowest since 1988 and an 82 TWh shortfall, driven by stress-corrosion outages at about a dozen reactors. Hydro output was also lower.
  - Fleet availability fell to just under 35% of capacity in August 2022 (GlobalData figure; treat with caution).
  - France was a **net electricity importer in 2022 for the first time since 1980**. EDF had to buy replacement power at record prices.
  - Sources: [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/021723-edfs-new-ceo-confirms-2023-french-nuclear-output-target-despite-further-delays); [Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/edfs-power-generation-france-reached-record-low-2022.html); [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/energy/electricity/frances-2022-electricity-generation-at-lowest-in-30-years-report/37529); [GlobalData](https://www.globaldata.com/data-insights/nuclear/frances-nuclear-power-generation-crisis-deepens/); [NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/nuclear-output-falls-due-to-impact-of-work-to-address-corrosion-problems-5-2-2022).
- **Nov and Dec 2024, Dunkelflaute in Germany:** prices above €300/MWh at times, peaking at **€936/MWh** on 12 Dec 2024. No market abuse was found (see §1) — [Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2025/20251021_Preisspitzen.html); [Fortune (12 Dec 2024)](https://fortune.com/europe/2024/12/12/weather-phenomenon-dunkelflaute-germany-energy-prices).
- **28 Apr 2025, Iberian blackout:**
  - Mainland Spain and Portugal blacked out, parts of them **for up to 16 hours**. The immediate cause was a **surge in voltage**, the first blackout of this kind and Europe's most significant in more than two decades.
  - **ENTSO-E's final report (20 Mar 2026)** blames a combination of oscillations; gaps in voltage and reactive-power control; differing voltage-regulation practices; rapid output reductions and generator disconnections in Spain; and uneven stabilisation capability.
  - Conventional plants reached under 75% of the reactive-power setpoints requested at critical moments. Some voltage-control equipment was switched manually, which slowed the response. Overvoltage protection settings did not meet requirements.
  - Many renewable plants ran on fixed power factor, and a significant number disconnected before reaching their specified voltage thresholds.
  - ENTSO-E chair Damián Cortinas: "The problem is not renewable energy, but voltage control, regardless of the type of generation."
  - Sources: [ENTSO-E blackout page](https://entsoe.eu/publications/blackout/28-april-2025-iberian-blackout); [Reuters via Internazionale (20 Mar 2026)](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/03/20/iberian-blackout-was-caused-by-multiple-factors-report-says); [pv magazine (23 Mar 2026)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/03/23/entso-e-report-finds-systemic-failures-behind-2025-iberian-blackout/); [The Local Spain](https://www.thelocal.es/20260320/how-the-perfect-storm-caused-spains-blackout); [heise](https://heise.de/-11224775); [Digital Substation, "90-second cascade"](https://digitalsubstation.com/en/taxonomy/regions/spain).

### Inferences
These are event-card designs grounded in the cited magnitudes; the effects are suggestions.
- **"Hurricane Lothar" (December 1999, France / Switzerland / south-west Germany):**
  - Wind and overhead lines in the affected regions go offline for days; transmission repairs cost money.
  - Secondary effect: a timber glut (200 million m³ felled) makes biomass fuel cheaper for 1–2 years.
  - Insurance can be a purchasable mitigation, since €8.6bn of the losses were insured.
- **"A tree on the Lukmanier line" (2003):** an import-dependent region blacks out for 12 hours. Local generation and storage earn a reliability bonus, and a later policy adds "N-1" grid rules.
- **"Cruise ship switching" (2006):** the grid splits into three islands. Plants with fast frequency response earn a payout; inflexible plants are tripped.
- **"Carbon crash" (2007):** the CO₂ price falls to about 0, coal margins rise and the green premium shrinks. It is reversed at the start of Phase 2 (January 2008, about €20/t).
- **"Negative-price day" (from October 2008):** unlocks a "curtail" action and a "pay to produce" risk for inflexible baseload. Its frequency should rise steeply after 2020 and reach about 16% of spring hours in high-solar markets by 2026.
- **"Subsidy cap reached" (Italy, 2013)** and **"Retroactive cut" (Spain, 2010–2013):** existing feed-in contracts are cut by a percentage. An "arbitration" mini-game pays out years later; Eiser won in 2017.
- **"Moonshot cancelled" (Makani 2020, Ampyx 2022, Pelamis 2014, FlagshipONE 2024):** each experimental tech gets a probability that its vendor collapses, stranding the player's investment.
- **"Gas shock" (2021–2022):** gas power costs multiply; wholesale prices above €500/MWh are possible, with futures above €1,000. This is followed by a windfall cap at €180/MWh (December 2022–June 2023) and, in Iberia, a gas cap (€40 → €65/MWh).
- **"Corrosion outages" (France, 2022):** about a third of the nuclear fleet is unavailable, the region becomes a net importer, and neighbours' prices spike. Exporters benefit.
- **"Serial defect" (2023):** a specific turbine model (a "4.X/5.X-like" platform) needs blade and bearing repairs. Owners of that model lose availability; the manufacturer rival takes a big loss.
- **"Offshore overreach" (2023–2025):** rising interest rates and supply-chain inflation make fixed-price offshore bids loss-making. Players can cancel and take write-downs; a political stop-work order can freeze a foreign project that is 80% complete; a rival can be forced into a rescue rights issue.
- **"Dunkelflaute" (2024):** wind and solar near zero for 2–3 days and prices up to about €936/MWh. This is the key payoff event for batteries, liquid-air storage and peakers.
- **"Grid resync" (Baltics, 2025):** a region runs islanded for about 24 hours. Frequency-reserve prices spike and it is risky for unprepared plants.
- **"Voltage collapse" (Iberia, 2025):** a blackout of up to 16 hours. Afterwards the regulator requires voltage-control and grid-forming capability, which unlocks a "grid-forming inverter" upgrade and penalises fixed-power-factor plants.
- **"Negative-price epidemic" (Q1 2026):** Spain has 347 negative hours in a quarter. Unhedged solar loses value and co-located storage gains.

## 4. Major European energy companies and their arcs (inspiration for AI rivals)

### Takeaway
The best-documented arc is **DONG → Ørsted**:
- 2006: a state oil-and-gas and coal company forms by merger.
- 1991 and 2002: offshore wind pioneer (Vindeby, Horns Rev).
- 2016: IPO. 2017: sells oil and gas and renames itself.
- 2023–2025: overreaches in the US and is rescued with DKK 60bn of new equity.

The equipment side shows Danish manufacturers consolidating: Vestas absorbed NEG Micon (2004) and Siemens bought Bonus (2004). Siemens Gamesa's 2023 quality crisis needed state guarantees. Incumbents' arcs (EDF's 2022 loss and EPR overruns, Uniper's coal exit, Vattenfall's switch to SMRs) are partly sourced. RWE, E.ON, Iberdrola, Enel and Statkraft could not be verified this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
These are fictionalised rival archetypes; each name is invented and the arc follows the sourced pattern.
- **"Nordhav Energi"** (pattern: DONG → Ørsted): a state fossil incumbent that wins early offshore demos (1991, 2002), IPOs, sells its oil and gas, renames itself, overextends abroad (2023), and then needs a huge rights issue (2025). Its behaviour: aggressive offshore bidding at fixed prices. Weakness: interest-rate and supply-chain shocks.
- **"Vindsmeden" (OEM)** (pattern: Vestas, NEG Micon, Bonus/Siemens): sells turbines to the player and rivals and grows by merger (2004). A "serial defect" event (pattern: Siemens Gamesa 2023) can hit a rival manufacturer, changing turbine prices and availability.
- **"Atomique Nationale"** (pattern: EDF): a nuclear national champion. Its fleet suffers correlated outages (2022 corrosion), and its new builds run over budget by 3–7 times (Flamanville 3, Hinkley Point C). Its behaviour: bids for very large CfD-backed projects.
- **"Kohle-Union"** (pattern: Uniper, with Ratcliffe and gas trading): a fossil and gas trader whose margins collapse in a supply shock. Its nationalisation is plausible history but unverified (see Gaps).
- **"Fjellkraft"** (pattern: Statkraft and Nordic hydro; unverified): a reservoir-hydro giant that profits from volatility and droughts by managing water value.
- **Start-up rivals or acquisition targets:** "Frostline Storage" (liquid air), "TowerVault" (gravity), "Sea Serpent" (wave) and "SkyKite" (airborne wind), each carrying an explicit bankruptcy risk calibrated to the real failures.
- **Independent-developer path** (pattern: European Energy and Danish cooperatives): the player's own trajectory, from community wind (1980s–90s), through solar parks, to PtX (2025).

## 5. Per-era summary: what an independent entrepreneur could build, how they sold power, and what returns were possible

### Takeaway
Sourced numbers sketch a clear arc of revenue models:
- **1990s:** small turbines on guaranteed purchase at 90% of the retail-derived rate (Germany) or cooperative ownership (Denmark).
- **2000s:** fixed 20-year tariffs, with extremely rich PV premiums (Italy's €0.445–0.49/kWh, about ten times today's PV cost).
- **2010s:** caps, retroactive cuts and integrated, coupled markets.
- **2020s:** merchant volatility (€936/MWh spikes against 16% negative hours), windfall caps and two-sided CfDs from 2027. Storage profits from the volatility; pack prices reached $70/kWh in 2025.
- **2030s and later:** long-duration storage, SMRs, energy islands and PtX are planned, while their economics are unproven.

No sourced IRR or return data was found, so the return bands below are labelled design suggestions.

### Cited Findings
- **1990s revenue model, Germany:** guaranteed purchase at **90%** of the retail-derived mean revenue per kWh for wind and solar; 65–75% for small hydro and biomass ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/policies/3477-electricity-feed-in-law-of-1991-stromeinspeisungsgesetz)).
- **1990s ownership model, Denmark:** cooperatives owned about 50% of Danish wind capacity from the 1980s to the early 1990s ([Green Economy Coalition](https://www.greeneconomycoalition.org/news-and-resources/people-power-denmarks-energy-cooperatives)). Middelgrunden was a 50/50 split between a cooperative of about 10,000 members and the utility ([Middelgrunden (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelgrunden_wind_farm)).
- **1990s wholesale model, UK and Nordics:** the GB Pool from 1990 was dominated by a duopoly; the regulator encouraged independent power projects and forced divestments ([ESD News](https://esdnews.com.au/the-rise-and-demise-of-electricity-competition/)). Nord Pool was the first multinational exchange (1996) ([Nord Pool](https://www.nordpoolgroup.com/globalassets/power-to-the-people---the-nordic-power-market-20-yrs.pdf)).
- **2000s revenue model:**
  - EEG fixed rates for 20 years ([wind-works.org](https://wind-works.org/evolution-of-feed-in-tariffs/)).
  - Italy's PV premium of €0.445–0.490/kWh, 2005–06 ([IEA](https://prod.iea.org/policies/4902-old-feed-in-premium-for-photovoltaic-systems-i-ii-iii-iv-conto-energia)).
  - Spain's RD 661/2007 rush, with €935m committed to large plants in 2007 ([Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/espana-pierde-su-primer-arbitraje-internacional-por-20170505)).
- **2010s subsidy compression:**
  - Italian tariffs fell from €0.22–0.34 (CE III) to €0.10–0.22/kWh (CE V) before the €6.7bn/yr cap closed the scheme in 2013 ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2012/07/17/is-italys-conto-energia-v-pointless_10007760/); [Solarserver](https://www.solarserver.de/2013/06/04/italiens-photovoltaik-einspeiseverguetung-wird-auslaufen-obergrenze-des-foerderprogramms-conto-energia-fast-erreicht/)).
  - Spain's retroactive cuts, 2010–2013 ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2013/02/21/spain-publishes-retroactive-pv-fit-cuts_100010298/)).
- **2020s price extremes:**
  - German day-ahead €552/MWh (17 Aug 2022) and French futures above €1,100/MWh ([AleaSoft](https://aleasoft.com/price-records-european-electricity-markets-after-highs-gas/)).
  - The revenue cap of €180/MWh from December 2022 to June 2023 ([Ashurst](https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/emergency-legal-measure-in-the-energy-sector/)).
  - The Dunkelflaute peak of €936/MWh in 2024 ([Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2025/20251021_Preisspitzen.html)).
  - 1,223 EU negative-price hours in Q1 2026, and 16% of hours negative in Spain ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/08/europes-negative-electricity-price-hours-double-in-q1-amid-renewables-surpluses-market-imbalances/)).
- **2020s cost base:** levelised cost in 2024 of $0.034/kWh (onshore), $0.043 (PV) and $0.057 (hydro). Installed costs of $691/kW (PV), $1,041/kW (onshore) and $2,852/kW (offshore) ([IRENA](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024)). Storage costs: see §2 ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/)).
- **2027 onward:** two-sided CfDs for newly subsidised low-carbon generation ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/energy-infrastructure/art/reform-european-electricity-market-design)); UK cap-and-floor support for long-duration storage ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250923277288/en/Highview-Power-Announces-Two-Long-Duration-Energy-Storage-Projects-Eligible-for-Support-Under-UK-Governments-Cap-and-Floor-Scheme)).
- **2030s planned assets:** Hinkley Point C (about 2030), SMRs (early 2030s), Bornholm (2034) — see §2.
- **PtX offtake risk:** FlagshipONE could not sign long-term contracts "at a viable price" ([Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/oersted-pulls-plug-on-shipping-e-methanol-fuel-project-citing-slower-demand)). Kassø did secure corporate offtakers (Maersk, LEGO, Novo Nordisk) ([LEGO Group](https://www.lego.com/aboutus/news/2025/may/kass-e-methanol-facility-officially-inaugurated)).

### Inferences
- **How rich the subsidies were** (computed from cited numbers):
  - Italy's first PV premium (€0.445–0.49/kWh) is **about 10 times** 2024's global PV levelised cost ($0.043/kWh), and **about 2.5 times** the €180/MWh level the EU treated as an excess "windfall" in 2022.
  - Italy's roughly €130bn lifetime cost spread over about 16.7 GW comes to **about €7.8bn per GW**. The €6.7bn/yr cap over the same capacity is about **€0.40 per W per year** of subsidy. Rooftop PV in 2005–2012 should therefore be the most lucrative small-player asset in the game, with a matching "retroactivity" risk in Spain-like markets.
- **Era guide for the game.** Return bands are **design suggestions, not sourced data**; tune them in playtesting.
