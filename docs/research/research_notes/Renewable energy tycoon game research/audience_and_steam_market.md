## 1. Who plays tycoon, management and economic sims on PC (demographics, motivations, behaviour of players aged 30+)

### Takeaway
The core buyer is most likely a male-leaning PC strategy or sim player aged about 30–55. These players are driven by strategy and planning, the one gamer motivation that does not fade with age, and they prefer non-confrontational goals (economic, scientific or cultural wins rather than conquest). City-building and management draw more women than grand strategy does (grand strategy is only 7% female). Two caveats. No current demographic dataset specific to tycoon games turned up. And Quantic Foundry finds that interest in strategic thinking among gamers as a whole has fallen steadily since 2015.

### Cited Findings
**Age and motivation (Quantic Foundry)**
- Strategy (thinking ahead, making complex decisions) is the most age-stable of Quantic Foundry's 12 motivations: it does not decline with age, while Competition declines the most. Men score higher on Strategy than women at every age, but only slightly. Men's higher competitiveness reaches parity with women's after about age 45. Sample: 140,000+ Gamer Motivation Profiles (published Feb 2016; STALE, but it's the only age-curve dataset I found). — [Quantic Foundry, 2016](https://quanticfoundry.com/2016/02/10/gamer-generation/); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/study-analyses-gaming-tastes-as-we-age); [Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/the-older-you-get-the-less-competitive-a-gamer-you-become/)
- In 4X games, players who pursue scientific or cultural victories average 28 years old, against 25 for those who pursue military or economic victories (26 for no preference). In Quantic Foundry's words: "younger and male gamers prefer military conquest; older and female gamers prefer cultural domination" (Mar 2016, STALE). — [Quantic Foundry, 2016](https://quanticfoundry.com/2016/03/02/4x-strategy/)
  - Caution: Quantic Foundry's survey sample skews young. A search summary of its GDC material gives a median respondent age of 23 (range 13–70). Absolute ages from QF therefore understate real buyer ages. — [QF GDC 2017 slides](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/GDC-2017-Slides-Quantic-Foundry.pdf) (I could not confirm which deck the figure comes from)
- **Strategy motivation is declining across the gamer population.** As of April 2024, the average Strategy score sat at the 33rd percentile of the June 2015 baseline, so 67% of gamers now care less about strategic thinking and planning than the average gamer did in 2015. It is the largest shift among the 12 motivations, and it began before COVID. It holds for men and women and across regions, except China, whose gamers "have a very different gaming motivation profile". Sample: 1.5M+ gamers over 9 years (published 21 May 2024). — [Quantic Foundry, May 2024](https://quanticfoundry.com/2024/05/21/strategy-decline/); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2024/05/bombshell-report-finds-players-becoming-less-interested-in-deep-strategy-games); [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240602-game-strategy-thinking-planning-decline/)
  - Counterpoint: Game Developer questioned the finding because strategy-game sales have held up ("If strategy game sales are steady, why are players 'less interested' in strategic thinking?"). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/if-strategy-game-sales-are-persistent-why-are-players-less-interested-in-strategic-thinking-)

**Gender**
- Across 23 genres (270,000 gamers, 18.5% of them female; Jan 2017, STALE), Grand Strategy had only 7% female players, among the lowest. Match-3 and Family/Farm Sim were the most female at 69%. Cities: Skylines drew a larger female share than Football Manager, Total War or Europa Universalis IV. — [Quantic Foundry, 2017](https://quanticfoundry.com/2017/01/19/female-gamers-by-genre/); [Game Developer repost](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/beyond-50-50-breaking-down-the-percentage-of-female-gamers-by-genre); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/study-shows-which-video-game-genres-women-play-most-1791435415)

**The ageing market overall**
- ESA (US): 205.1M Americans play, with an average age of 36, and half of players are 35 or older. Nearly half of boomers (ages 61–79) play weekly. Later coverage reports 212M weekly US players with an average age of 37, "up from 29 about two decades ago". Another headline pairs 212M with an average age of 36. Which report version and year each figure comes from is unverified; the 37 figure is probably from 2026. — [ESA](https://www.theesa.com/annual-esa-study-reveals-video-games-universal-appeal-across-generations/); [Feedbagel (212M, age 37)](https://feedbagel.com/post/nearly-70-of-americans-play-video-games-for-at-least-an-hour-each-week-new-repor); [Feedbagel (212M, age 36)](https://feedbagel.com/post/major-industry-report-reveals-212-million-americans-officially-gamers); [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/sponsored/spotlight/average-age-of-american-video-game-players-rises-to-37-new-industry-report-finds)
- Global (ESA 2025 global study, as summarised): the average player worldwide is 41. Ages 25–44 are the largest group (49%), and there are more players aged 45–54 (16%) than 16–24 (14%). Players over 55 lean toward single-player games and puzzles. Covers all platforms, including mobile. — [Guru3D](https://www.guru3d.com/story/global-gamers-are-getting-older-average-gamer-age-now-41-worldwide/); [GameDev Reports: ESA Global Video Games Report 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/esa-global-video-games-report-2025)
- Paradox Interactive's target audience for grand strategy is "35-plus males". Paradox reaches them mostly through its own channels and spends more marketing money on community support and retention than on acquisition (DFC Intelligence; date not shown). Investor commentary (opinion) describes Paradox as riding the "megatrend of player aging", with players "willing to pay a lot for their games". — [DFC Intelligence](https://www.dfcint.com/paradoxs-grand-strategy/); [Inderes investor forum](https://forum.inderes.com/t/paradox-interactive-ab-jos-viela-yksi-lisaosa/5213?tl=en)
- Size of the PC audience: Newzoo's 2025 Growth Report counts 936M PC players worldwide (secondary source quoting Christopher Dring). — [NeoGAF thread quoting Newzoo/Dring](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/dring-newzoo-2025-growth-report-consoles-2-5-mobile-4-5-pc-3-1-console-645m-gamers-pc-936m-gamers.1688077/)
- Newzoo-attributed figure (exact report and year unverified): millennials gravitate toward MMO (41%), strategy (27%) and RPG (26%). — [GameDev Reports: Newzoo American Player Portrait 2022](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/newzoo-american-player-portrait-in)

**How deeply players engage in a realism-heavy niche**
- Nucleares (nuclear-reactor operation sim): median playtime 6.8 h, mean 21.5 h, and 86% of owners played at least 1 h (third-party Steam analytics; date not shown). The gap between median and mean points to a long tail of very deep players. — [steamdata.ai](https://steamdata.ai/game/1428420); [nodal.gg](https://nodal.gg/game/nucleares-1428420)

### Inferences
- The likely primary demographic for Gridmaster is male PC strategy and sim players aged about 30–55. A meaningful female minority is plausible if the game reads as "building and management" (like Cities: Skylines) rather than "grand strategy or war" (7% female). Leaving out military framing also fits older players' preference for non-confrontational goals.
- Pausable, planning-heavy, economic play fits the one motivation that stays stable with age (Strategy). The population-wide decline in Strategy (QF 2024) argues for strong onboarding, short feedback loops and optional depth ("casual on the surface, realistic underneath") rather than a spreadsheet-first design.
- Paradox's case suggests older strategy players stay loyal and buy DLC, and are reached through community channels more than paid ads. That supports a dev-log and Discord-first marketing plan with DLC or expansions later.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 breakdown of age, gender or region specific to buyers of tycoon or management games on Steam. Quantic Foundry's genre-by-gender data dates from 2017 and its age curves from 2016. I could not confirm QF's female share for "City Building".
- No sourced data on how 30+ players behave: session length, preference for pausable play, willingness to pay, or how often they write reviews.
- Newzoo's paywalled PC genre-by-age data was not accessible.

## 2. Steam market size and performance for relevant tags

### Takeaway
Steam is saturated. About 19,000–20,500 games came out in 2025, about 3% reached 1,000 reviews, and the median game earns roughly $1k–$5k over its lifetime. The building/strategy/sim cluster is still one of the better-performing parts of Steam: GameDiscoverCo found median revenues of about $22k for city builders, $35k for 4X and $44k for colony sims, against a general baseline of about $5k. "Management" as a tag has a lower 1,000-review hit rate than city builders, colony sims or survival games. Most games that leave Early Access earn less at 1.0 than they did at their Early Access launch.

### Cited Findings
**Release volume**
- Steam releases in 2025 by tracker: GameDiscoverCo 20,558, Gamalytic 20,353, VG Insights 20,282, SteamDB about 19,000–20,008. Counts differ by method. Prior years as summarised: 18,558 in 2024 and 14,111 in 2023 (published 8 Jan 2026). — [How To Market A Game, 8 Jan 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/08/how-many-games-were-released-in-2025/); [GamingOnLinux, Dec 2025](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews/page=1/)
- Nearly half of 2025's releases had fewer than 10 reviews. — [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/18k-games-released-steam-2025-half-have-10-reviews); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews/page=1/)
- First half of 2025 alone: about 9,500 new releases. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-48224)

**Hit rates (Chris Zukowski's measure: the share of releases that reach 1,000 Steam reviews)**
- 608 of 20,282 games released in 2025 (2.99%) reached 1,000 reviews, the highest rate in four years (2024: 2.44%; 2023: 2.56%; 2022: 2.74%). 579 of the 608 were AA or indie games. Zukowski: "We might be in a bit of an indie golden age." Most common genres among 2025 games with 1,000+ reviews: Narrative 51, Simulation 43, Horror 39 (published 27 Jan 2026). — [HTMAG, "What the hell happened in 2025?"](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/); [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/)
- Genre "success rates" for 2025, as summarised from the same Zukowski post: Job simulator 34.7%, Open-world survival 20.8%, **City builder 6.4%, Management 3.4%**. I could not confirm from the page what each percentage is a share of. — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/). **Contradicted** by another summary claiming only 0.5% of 2025 city builders reached 1,000 reviews (down from 2.70% in 2024) and that "management" ranked 19th among genres for breakout hits in 2025. That summary's source is unclear; it cited [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/indie-game-statistics-2026/) and [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-revenue-data).
- 2024: the Simulation hit rate was reported as 3.76%, but another summarised figure gives 27 of 278 Simulation games (9.71%) reaching 1,000+ reviews. The two conflict, probably because they define the tag differently. Simulation has been in the top 10 genres for "success" for three years running. — [HTMAG, Jan 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/01/15/what-the-hell-happened-in-2024/)
- A third-party niche report (Sept 2026, "Strategy & management") says 11.6% of 2024–25 games in its cohort reached 1,000+ reviews and 46.8% reached 100+. Its denominator is probably filtered, and its method is unknown. — [Steam Page Analyzer niche report](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/niches/colony-sim)

**Revenue**
- GameDiscoverCo median Steam revenue by tag: **Colony Sim $44k (292 games), Roguelike Deckbuilder $38k, 4X $35k, City Builder $22k, Grand Strategy $19k**, against roughly $5k as a general baseline. Simon Carless says these are tags which, "if they truly apply to your game," have some of the best median results on Steam. The analysis date is unconfirmed: a Gamasutra-era repost suggests about 2021, so this may be STALE. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/which-genre-should-your-next-pc-game); [Game Developer repost](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/which-genre-should-your-next-pc-game-be-in-2); [Jioforme repost of the Gamasutra blog](https://www.jioforme.com/gamasutra-simon-carlesss-blog-which-genre-should-the-next-pc-game-be-in/221585/)
- Survival-crafting, colony sim, 4X, city builder and management games have the highest median revenues of the genres on Steam (third-party summary, 2026). — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/median-steam-game-2026)
- The median Steam game grosses about **$1,000 over its lifetime**, never reaches 10 reviews, and is priced at a level "drifting down toward $15". Median revenue is $3,285 for self-published games and $16,222 for publisher-backed games, about 5× more (third-party, 2026; method not inspected). — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/median-steam-game-2026); [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/indie-game-statistics-2026/)
- Gamalytic: 67% of Steam games earned less than $5k (date unconfirmed). — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamalytic-67-of-games-on-steam-earned)
- VG Insights (Jan–Aug 2020 data, STALE): indie Simulation and Strategy games had nearly identical revenue spreads. 11% earned $0, 43% earned $0–10k, 19–20% earned $10–50k, 12% earned $50–200k, and **14–15% earned over $200k**. — [Statista / VG Insights](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1356730/steam-indie-game-revenue-genre/)
- VG Insights: indie games took 48% of Steam's full-game revenue through Sept 2024, the first time indies matched AAA and AA combined, and 58% of copies sold. This uses a broad tag-based definition of "indie" that includes Palworld. — [VG Insights Global Indie Games Market Report 2024](https://app.sensortower.com/vgi/assets/reports/VGI_Global_Indie_Games_Market_Report_2024.pdf)
- Zukowski calls "crafty building strategy simulation-y games" a consistently successful Steam cluster: sandbox-like games that sell well "even if they don't become household names". Data from February 2026 showed survival-craft games and this cluster had "no signs of cooling off". In his sales analysis the leading genres are RPG, Builders, Rogue-likes and Strategy. — [Valadria podcast with Zukowski](https://www.valadria.com/steam-game-marketing-with-chris-zukowski/); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/blog/steam-next-fest-2026-results); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-44107)

**Early Access (EA)**
- EA games were slightly over 14% of new Steam releases in 2024 (2,897 titles) and about 18% in 2023; the share usually sits between 13% and 19%. More than half of EA games leave EA within 12 months, about a quarter stay around a year, and a substantial share stay up to two years. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-47434)
- GameDiscoverCo: of 225 games that left EA in 2025, **only 20% (45) earned more at 1.0 than in EA**. Median time in EA was 437 days. The median game's first 30 days at 1.0 earned about 40% of what its first 30 days in EA earned. Niche Gamer gives the share as 21%. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-early-access-graduates); [Niche Gamer](https://nichegamer.com/new-data-shows-only-21-of-early-access-games-perform-better-after-full-release/); [games.gg](https://games.gg/news/steam-early-access-graduates-in-2025/)
- 28% of 2024's top-grossing new Steam releases were EA games (search summary; underlying source unconfirmed). — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-47434)
- Historical: EEDAR (2014, STALE) found only 25% of EA games had reached full release. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/only-25-percent-of-early-access-games-have-made-it-to-full-release-eedar-says/)

### Inferences
- Realistic management and sim games are a better-than-average bet on Steam, but "Management" or "Tycoon" alone look weaker than "City Builder", "Colony Sim" or survival-crafting. Gridmaster should earn adjacent tags honestly (Building, Resource Management, Economy, Simulation, Strategy, perhaps City Builder-like map building). Tags must genuinely apply, as Carless warns.
- **Average revenue per copy for comparables.** Dividing each title's estimated gross revenue by its estimated copies gives a blended figure after discounts and regional pricing, before Steam's cut, computed from the section 3 figures. Software Inc. ≈ $11.9, Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 ≈ $15.0, Mad Games Tycoon 2 ≈ $16.7, Workers & Resources ≈ $24.1, Rise of Industry ≈ €11.4 across all platforms, and Terra Nil ≈ $20 in its first month. That range supports a list price of about $15–25.
- Since most games earn less at 1.0 than at their EA launch, the EA launch effectively *is* the launch. Gridmaster should enter EA only with a substantial, polished slice, for example with the 1990s–2010s eras complete, and treat 1.0 as a smaller second launch.
- With about 20k releases a year and about a 3% hit rate, a realistic base case for a solo developer's first sim is in the low tens of thousands of dollars (tag medians are $20–45k). There is a fat upside tail, with comparables at $3–15M, but reaching it depends on the hook and the wishlist count.

### Gaps
- I could not query Gamalytic, VG Insights or SteamDB for current (2025–26) tag-level figures: release counts, median and top-decile revenue for Management, Tycoon, Economy and Resource Management. No top-decile revenue by tag was found.
- I could not confirm from the primary pages the date and definitions behind GameDiscoverCo's tag medians or the meaning of Zukowski's 2025 genre percentages.
- No systematic dataset of typical price points for indie tycoon games was found, only individual prices (see section 3).

## 3. How comparable titles performed

### Takeaway
Small-team economic sims can do very well, and two Danish small or solo studios prove it: Hovgaard Games' Big Ambitions reportedly sold 1.1M copies, and Coredumping's one-man Software Inc. an estimated ~295k. Realism-heavy sims (Workers & Resources, Transport Fever 2, Farming Simulator, Car Mechanic Simulator, Nucleares) show strong appetite for authenticity. Outcomes vary widely, though: Offworld Trading Company sold about 100k, and Rise of Industry sold 350k+ copies yet its developer ended up in deficit.

These are players, not sales; the count includes Game Pass and PS Plus. — [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/75072/powerwash-simulator-reaches-17m-players/); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/powerwash-simulator-player-count)
- **Car Mechanic Simulator 2021** (Red Dot Games / PlayWay, Poland): 132k PC copies plus 11k on Xbox in 3 days (Aug 2021). Its 2.7M PLN development and marketing budget was recouped within 24 hours. Estimated **838.8k Steam copies and $12.6M gross** (estimate as of July 2026). — [Bankier](https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/Sukces-Car-Mechanic-Simulator-2021-od-PlayWay-8169155.html); [PlayWay post-launch report](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/raport-bie-cy-nr-19-2021-popremierowy-raport-sprzeda-owy-dotycz-cy-gry-car-mechanic-simulator-2021); [Raijin.gg](https://raijin.gg/app/1190000/Car_Mechanic_Simulator_2021)
- PlayWay: House Flipper and Car Mechanic Simulator together brought in half of one quarter's revenue. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/finance/item-43605)

**Publisher benchmark**
- Hooded Horse (a strategy and management publisher) reported 9.5M wishlists and 4.5M copies sold across its catalogue in one year (date unconfirmed). — [WN Hub (Russian)](https://wnhub.io/ru/news/stores-and-publishing/item-45773)

### Inferences
- Denmark already has two proof points for small-team management sims (Hovgaard, Coredumping). That helps with press ("a Danish tycoon tradition") and with setting expectations. Software Inc.'s estimated ~$3.5M across many years of EA is a strong *solo* outcome. Big Ambitions' 1M+ relied on a mass-appeal hook (life sim plus business).
- Realism sells when it is tactile and easy to read (Farming Simulator, Car Mechanic Simulator, PowerWash, Nucleares). Gridmaster's "real-data-inspired weather and prices" should be *shown* in trailers, for example weather fronts sweeping across wind farms and price charts reacting, rather than just described.
- Rise of Industry is a warning that copies sold are not the same as developer income: publisher splits and costs can leave a team in deficit even at 350k copies. A solo hobbyist should compare self-publishing with a publisher on fair terms before signing.
- Offworld shows that a combat-free economic strategy game, even from a famous designer, can stall at around 100k copies when its hook is abstract. Gridmaster's concrete theme (energy, Europe, history) is an advantage over pure market abstraction.

### Gaps
- No verified sales or revenue for Turmoil, Production Line, Kapital, Game Dev Tycoon (lifetime), Terra Nil (lifetime) or Nucleares (copies). Team sizes and development times are mostly missing because Gamalytic and VG Insights pages and postmortems couldn't be opened.
- The third-party estimates (Raijin, steamdata.ai, Vorythic/Grokipedia, Gamesensor) use unknown methods. The Mad Games Tycoon 2 estimate looks low for how popular the game is, but that is an unverified impression.

I found little hard, current data. What exists shows three things. Several of the deepest building and simulator titles come from Central European studios (Slovak and Polish, among others), and the niche gets German-language press coverage. Polish publishers have industrialised the "simulator" genre. Chinese players have a distinct motivation profile; the strategy decline was not observed there. I could not confirm localisation priorities from sources; they are listed under Gaps.

That shows a German-language editorial interest in the niche. — [GIGA](https://www.giga.de/games/75-prozent-guenstiger-steam-bietet-originelle-aufbau-sim-zum-sparpreis-an--01J5QXYRRHDTKKN2XTNPSC5PGB)

## 5. Theme fit: how climate, energy and "green" games have performed

### Takeaway
Environmental themes sell when framed as competent, optimistic building (Terra Nil, Eco) or as gripping survival and moral drama (Frostpunk, 5M+ copies). The one explicit climate-policy simulation, Fate of the World (2011), was praised but never disclosed sales. I found no evidence on what triggers backlash, or on whether interest in energy rose after the 2022 energy crisis.

### Cited Findings
- **Terra Nil** (a "reverse city builder" about restoring ecosystems; Free Lives and Devolver; launched 28 Mar 2023): 300k players in its first week across Steam, GOG, Epic and the Netflix-subscriber mobile version. Sales are undisclosed. One third-party estimate puts its first month at about $1M revenue and 50k+ copies. The studio donates 8% of profits to the Endangered Wildlife Trust and had funded 45,528 trees through Tree-Nation. — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/04/06/terra-nil-300000-players-free-lives-tree-planting); [Gamesensor](https://gamesensor.info/news/terra_nil_sales); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Nil)
- **Eco** (Strange Loop Games; a cooperative civilisation sim where pollution and over-harvesting have lasting consequences): over 400k copies on Steam; SteamSpy estimates 0.5–1M owners. Its 2015 Kickstarter raised over $200k against a $100k goal, and it entered EA in Feb 2018. A version was funded by a US Department of Education grant (SBIR) for teaching environmental literacy to middle-school students. In 2017 the studio publicly "challenged climate deniers". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco_(2018_video_game)); [IES / US Dept of Education](https://ies.ed.gov/sbir/ECO.asp); [Cliqist](https://cliqist.com/2017/04/26/strange-loop-games-challenges-climate-deniers-eco/); [SteamSpy](https://steamspy.com/dev/Strange+Loop+Games)
- **Fate of the World** (Red Redemption, 2011, £9.99): a climate-policy strategy game based on scientific research. Gaming experts hailed it as a potential breakthrough for social-change games, and climate campaigners welcomed it. Sales were never disclosed. The studio's earlier BBC browser climate game had been played over 1M times. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate_of_the_World); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/climate-change-game-worlds-fate-rests-gamers-hands); [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/global-warming-game-finds-publisher/)
- **Frostpunk** (a survival city builder set in a climate catastrophe): 250k copies in 3 days (2018), 3M within 3 years, **over 5M by Apr 2024**. **Frostpunk 2**: 350k in 4 days (Sept 2024), 592k by 31 Dec 2024, and over 1M later. It had earned 11 bit studios PLN 116.17M (~$30.8M) by 31 Mar 2026 and covered its production and marketing costs. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/investment/item-46200); [WN Hub (1M)](https://wnhub.io/news/finance/item-51386); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/finance/item-47593); [Game8](https://indie.game8.co/news/frostpunk-2-reaches-350000-copies-sold/); [Fullcleared](https://fullcleared.com/news/frostpunk-2-is-already-profitable/)
- **Nucleares** (nuclear-reactor operation sim, MicroProse) is rated "Very Positive" (84%) on about 1.1k reviews. That shows a niche for technical realism about nuclear power with no political framing. — [steamdata.ai](https://steamdata.ai/game/1428420)

### Inferences
- The framings that work are competence and optimism (Terra Nil's restoration fantasy, Eco's systemic consequences) and drama (Frostpunk), not lecturing. A profit-driven tycoon in which renewables win because they become cheaper and better, with history as the teacher, fits the competence-and-optimism lane and works for players of any political leaning.
- Treat nuclear as a technology with trade-offs (cost, build time, and public acceptance as a game mechanic), not as a moral position. Nucleares shows that nuclear realism has an audience.
- Fate of the World suggests that casting the player as a policymaker ("save the planet by setting carbon taxes") earns praise but limited sales. Gridmaster's company-owner framing is the more commercial choice.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced evidence of player backlash against preachy or political climate games, nor of nuclear-specific controversy such as review bombing.
- I found no data on whether interest in energy topics rose after the 2022 European energy crisis (Google Trends, Steam tag growth). UNVERIFIED lead: Europe's record solar installations in 2022 (SolarPower Europe reported roughly 41 GW in the EU, about +47%) could serve as a proxy for public interest.
- Lifetime revenue for Terra Nil and Eco is unverified.
- I didn't survey other energy-themed games on Steam (small "power grid" or "energy tycoon" titles), so the scan of direct competitors is missing.

## 6. Marketing: wishlists, Next Fest, creators, Discord, Early Access vs full launch, and AI disclosure

### Takeaway
Benchmarks: about 7k wishlists to appear in Steam's Popular Upcoming list, 10k+ is "healthy" and 25k+ "strong". Next Fest is crowded (over 3,500 demos in Feb 2026), and most wishlists gained during Next Fest come from people who never play the demo. So the store page, trailer and capsule art matter more than the demo itself. I could not research which YouTubers and streamers cover the genre, or Steam's AI disclosure rules and player sentiment, because the search budget ran out; leads are under Gaps.

### Cited Findings
- About **7,000 wishlists**, gathered within a short period, is roughly the threshold for appearing on Steam's "Popular Upcoming" list. The common rule of thumb at launch: 7k is "risky but possible", **10k+ "healthy"**, 25k+ "strong" (2026 guides citing Zukowski and industry consensus). — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch); [Althera Games](https://altheragames.com/en/blog/steam-wishlist-guide)
- Zukowski has surveyed hundreds of developers after every Next Fest since 2022. In Feb 2025, **68–88% of wishlists came from people who never played the demo**. The Feb 2026 Next Fest had over 3,500 demos, 19% more than Oct 2025 and 51% more than a year earlier. — [presskit.gg: analysing Next Fest results](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/analyzing-next-fest-results); [presskit.gg: Next Fest 2026 results](https://presskit.gg/blog/steam-next-fest-2026-results); [Ziva](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026)
- Zukowski: "The moment you say 'I'm gonna make this type of game,' you've actually made the biggest marketing decision." Sandbox-like building/strategy/sim games convert well because Steam's audience actively looks for them. — [Valadria podcast](https://www.valadria.com/steam-game-marketing-with-chris-zukowski/); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/blog/steam-next-fest-2026-results)
- Self-published median revenue is $3,285 against $16,222 for publisher-backed games (third-party, 2026). This is correlation, not proof that a publisher causes the difference. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/median-steam-game-2026)
- Paradox reaches its 35+ audience mostly through its own channels, putting more money into community support and retention than into acquisition. — [DFC Intelligence](https://www.dfcint.com/paradoxs-grand-strategy/)
- Big Ambitions' EA launch (100k copies in 7 days) shows that a small Danish studio can open big in EA when the hook is broad. — [Hovgaard Games on X](https://x.com/hovgaardgames/status/1636768237776928769)
- Early Access outcomes: see section 2 (only ~20% of 2025 EA graduates earned more at 1.0; median time in EA 437 days). — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-early-access-graduates)

### Inferences
- Aim for at least 10k wishlists, ideally 20k+, before an EA launch. Point the store page and trailer at the "grow a few solar panels into a European energy empire" fantasy. Use one Next Fest, with a short and polished demo, to multiply wishlists rather than as the only driver.
- Community: a dev-log and Discord approach (Paradox-style) suits older players who stay with a game. The "real data" angle (actual weather and price inspiration, historical events) can supply recurring content.
- AI: Players can't see AI coding assistance; they can see AI-generated art, voices and text. The lowest-risk route is human-made or licensed art and audio, plus open communication about how AI was used. Check Steam's current rules before launch (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- **YouTubers and streamers** who cover tycoon and management games, and what attracts them, were not researched. UNVERIFIED leads to confirm: English-language creators Nookrium, Splattercat, Aavak, Wanderbots, Orbital Potato and Biffa; German-language creator Steinwallen.
- **Steam's AI-content disclosure rules and 2025–26 player sentiment** were not researched. UNVERIFIED leads from background knowledge (mid-2026); verify before using:
  - Valve's January 2024 policy requires developers to disclose "pre-generated" and "live-generated" AI content in the Steamworks content survey, and the disclosure is shown on the store page. An update reported in January 2026 apparently clarified that AI tools used for development efficiency (such as coding assistants) are not what the disclosure is about; content that ships in the game or appears in marketing is.
  - Reported 2025 controversies in the same genre: Jurassic World Evolution 3 (Frontier) removed AI-generated portraits after player backlash (about Oct 2025), and Anno 117: Pax Romana (Ubisoft) shipped AI-generated art that Ubisoft called a placeholder that slipped through (about Nov 2025). Outside the genre, Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 reportedly lost its Indie Game Awards honours over generative-AI placeholder textures (Dec 2025).
  - An analysis by Totally Human (mid-2025) reportedly found about 7–8k Steam games disclosing generative-AI use, roughly 7% of the catalogue and about 1 in 5 new 2025 releases.
- No ratios for converting wishlists into first-week sales were found.
- No Discord community benchmarks were found.

## 7. Synthesis: personas, price, positioning, risks

### Takeaway
Primary audience: male-leaning European and North American PC strategy/sim players aged 30–55 who like realistic, pausable economic building. Secondary audiences: fans of realistic simulators, and energy-curious players. Suggested price: about $19.99/€19.99, with an EA range of $14.99–19.99 rising to $19.99–24.99 at 1.0. Positioning: a competent, optimistic, profit-driven historical energy tycoon. The biggest risks: discoverability, the weaker hit rate of the "management" tag, balancing depth against accessibility, politicisation of the theme, how AI use is perceived, revenue declining from EA to 1.0, and scope for a solo developer.

### Cited Findings
Key figures behind the recommendations, each sourced above:
- Strategy is the most age-stable motivation, older players prefer non-military victories, and grand strategy is 7% female. — [Quantic Foundry 2016](https://quanticfoundry.com/2016/02/10/gamer-generation/); [Quantic Foundry 4X](https://quanticfoundry.com/2016/03/02/4x-strategy/); [Quantic Foundry 2017](https://quanticfoundry.com/2017/01/19/female-gamers-by-genre/)
- Strategy motivation has declined population-wide since 2015 (to the 33rd percentile). — [Quantic Foundry 2024](https://quanticfoundry.com/2024/05/21/strategy-decline/)
- About 20k Steam releases in 2025 and a 2.99% hit rate; city builder 6.4% versus management 3.4% (definition unconfirmed). — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/)
- Tag medians: colony sim $44k, 4X $35k, city builder $22k, against a ~$5k baseline (date unconfirmed). — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/which-genre-should-your-next-pc-game)
- Only about 20% of 2025 EA graduates earned more at 1.0. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-early-access-graduates)
- Danish precedents: Big Ambitions reportedly 1.1M copies; Software Inc. an estimated ~$3.5M gross. — [Hovgaard news](https://www.hovgaard.com/news/); [Vorythic](https://vorythic.com/games/2856/software-inc)
- Wishlist benchmarks: 7k / 10k / 25k. — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch)

### Inferences
**Personas**
1. **Primary, "The Planner"**: about 35–55, mostly male, in DACH, the Nordics, Benelux, the UK or North America; often an engineer or works in IT or finance. Plays Anno, Transport Fever 2, Cities: Skylines, Workers & Resources and Factorio-likes. Plays 1–2 hour evening sessions and pauses a lot. Will pay full price for a well-reviewed game and buys DLC. Wants authenticity, charts and "historically right" progress. Dislikes grind, fake-feeling numbers and preaching. (Based on the Quantic Foundry age and victory-type data, Paradox's 35+ audience, and the success of Transport Fever 2 and Workers & Resources.)
2. **Secondary, "The Sim Hobbyist"**: a fan of realistic simulators (Farming Simulator, Car Mechanic Simulator, Nucleares) who enjoys running a concrete system more than abstract strategy. Hook: "run a real-feeling wind farm or grid", with tactile feedback. (Based on Farming Simulator 25 selling 4M, Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 ~839k estimated, and Nucleares' 84% positive rating.)
3. **Secondary, "The Energy-Curious"**: works in or follows the energy transition and may be newer to tycoon games. The theme is the reason to buy, and these players spread word in professional and enthusiast circles. Their gender mix is probably more balanced than the Planner's. (By analogy with Terra Nil's and Eco's theme-driven reach; no direct data.)
4. **Optional, Chinese sim players**: worth testing if a Simplified Chinese translation is affordable (based on Quantic Foundry's distinct China profile; size unsourced).

**Price**: launch EA at $14.99–19.99 (€ equivalent), rising to $19.99–24.99 at 1.0. Avoid going below $10, which signals low ambition and leaves little room for discounts. Basis: Kapital at $19.99, the median Steam price "drifting toward $15", and comparable titles averaging about $12–24 in realised revenue per copy (section 2).

**One-sentence positioning**: *"Gridmaster is a pausable, real-data-inspired energy tycoon in which you grow a handful of 1990s solar panels into Europe's leading power empire — reading the weather and the markets, keeping the lights on and the books in the black as wind, batteries, hydro, nuclear and tomorrow's tech reshape the grid."* No lecture: renewables win because the player makes them pay.

**Biggest market risks**
1. **Discoverability**: about 20k releases a year, roughly a 3% chance of reaching 1,000 reviews, and a median lifetime gross near $1k. A solo developer without a sharp hook and 10k+ wishlists will likely land at or below the tag medians.
2. **Genre weakness**: "management" appears to hit less often (3.4%) than city builder (6.4%) or survival and colony sims, and strategic motivation is declining population-wide. Mitigate with visual building and map expansion (city-builder appeal), satisfying growth loops and a strong early game.
3. **"Casual but realistic"**: too much spreadsheet loses the casual buyer (the strategy decline); too little realism loses the Planner. Workers & Resources shows hardcore realism can sell, but as a niche.
4. **Politicised theme**: climate and nuclear can come across as preachy or partisan. Keep the framing profit-and-competence based and present nuclear as trade-offs. Note: I found no data on how large this risk is.
5. **Perception of AI-assisted development**: possible backlash if players see AI-generated art, voices or text; disclosure obligations on Steam (UNVERIFIED details; see section 6). Keep player-facing assets human-made or licensed and be open about AI coding help.
6. **EA dynamics**: most games earn less at 1.0, and a median 437 days in EA demands a steady update cadence that is hard for a hobbyist.
7. **Business terms**: Rise of Industry sold 350k copies yet its studio ended in deficit. Read publisher splits carefully, or self-publish.
8. **Big-franchise competition and naming/IP**: Anno, Cities: Skylines and Transport Fever 3 (in development per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Fever_2)) compete for the same players' time. Avoid real energy-company names and logos.

### Gaps
- None of the personas could be checked against current demographic data specific to the genre (sections 1 and 4).
- The price recommendation rests on a few prices and estimates, not on a systematic price survey of the Management and Tycoon tags.
- The AI-disclosure and creator-outreach parts of the plan rest on unverified leads and need a follow-up research pass with working search and fetch access.
